- business_capture: <purpose>
Capture this node's BUSINESS LOGIC from current DDL so a business analyst
can understand: what changes, why the rule exists, how the rule is decided,
and what assumptions/effects exist both upstream and downstream.
</purpose>

⛔ STRICT GROUNDING — analyze only `bb_ddl` from the focus node. Use only
rules, formulas, thresholds, and relationships present in DDL. If a
construct is absent from DDL, treat it as out of scope. Quoted SQL
witnesses must be exact substrings of that DDL — never paraphrased SQL.
When the DDL proves the mechanism but not the intent, prevalence, or
runtime impact, state `not established from the available SQL` — never
fill a gap with a plausible inference.

<scope_relevance>
Logging, error-capture, audit, and retention sinks are usually not part of
the business answer — prune them (verdict: prune) unless the question is
specifically about logging, errors, auditing, or retention. Keep any node
that transforms or carries a value on the answer path.
</scope_relevance>

Submit one section: `{ angle: "business", text: "<body>" }`. The
archive has no length cap and synthesis lifts your text verbatim —
write at the depth the node's role in answering the user's question
warrants. Nodes that directly address the question get full depth;
context nodes in scope but tangential to the question get a brief
functional note. Brevity here becomes brevity in the final report.
A node central to the question typically needs several paragraphs
covering every rule and branch visible in its DDL — a 2–3 sentence
summary is under-depth for such a node.

<emphasis>
Highest-value content, in order:
1. **Business outcome** — what changes for users, reporting, or decisions.
2. **Business intent (why)** — the decision objective this rule serves.
3. **Decision rules (how)** — what condition triggers the change and what outcome it drives.
4. **Lifecycle impact** — business status/phase transitions when present.
5. **Audit-trail meaning** — what business event each audit entry represents.
6. **Material risks** — add ⚠️ only for significant, decision-impacting
   data-quality issues grounded in the loaded evidence.
7. **Technical evidence** — formulas, predicates, thresholds, and key column references
   used to explain business logic.
</emphasis>

<body_recipe>
1. Open with one sentence naming the domain role ("computes revenue
   at INSERT") and the decision objective ("why this rule exists").
2. Walk through business rules in plain language first. Name what
   changes for the business when each rule fires.
3. For EVERY computed or derived expression in the DDL (arithmetic,
   CASE result, COALESCE fallback, aggregation), write the formula as
   a `$$ … $$` block and explain the business meaning of its terms
   briefly.
4. Capture both **upstream assumptions** and **downstream effects** when
   evidenced in current DDL context.
5. Express status-enum lifecycles as numbered transitions
   ("1. New → 2. Approved → 3. Shipped"). Capture the business
   state change, not the SQL UPDATE that effects it.
6. Add ⚠️ only when the issue is significant and decision-impacting.
   If no material issue exists, omit risk callouts.
7. For SELECT-only views (no INSERT/UPDATE/DELETE): describe what data
   this view makes queryable and its business meaning — not how the
   SELECT is structured.
8. Wrap column and object names in `\`backticks\`` only when used.
</body_recipe>
